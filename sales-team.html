Building a sales team that delivers: hiring, training and performance systems for a luxury jewellery retailer

Role
Program Manager (Lead – CRM & Talent Management)
Timeline
Nov 2025 – Jul 2026
Scale
30 sales team members supported · 7 hires through a process I owned · Sales, Floor Managers, HR and Senior Leadership stakeholders
2 weeksfor new hires to become productive
−27%escalations
2 batchestraining model, no disruption to store hours

The challenge

The sales team was under pressure from several directions at once:

  • Overworked staff had less time for customers, which meant lost sales opportunities.
  • No structured training meant new hires struggled to learn the process and took a long time to become effective.
  • Inconsistent performance tracking gave no reliable view of who was performing well, who needed support, or where clients were being lost.

At the same time, the business was moving its sales team onto a new CRM and digital processes. Without a well-trained, well-supported team, the transformation itself was at risk.

My role

I owned talent management for the sales function, working with the store sales team, floor managers, HR and senior leadership. My responsibilities covered:

  • Workforce planning and hiring: defining role requirements, sourcing and interviewing candidates, and recommending them for final leadership and HR rounds
  • Hiring design: job descriptions, interview rounds, qualification criteria and assessments
  • Training: SOPs, the L&D curriculum and training delivery
  • Performance tracking, reviews and improvement plans
  • Conflict resolution and day-to-day team support

The approach

1. Hiring for the gap, not just the vacancy

Before hiring, I worked with each stakeholder group to understand what the business actually needed. One key insight: the existing sales team was strong at selling but not comfortable with technology. Instead of forcing a difficult skill change on everyone at once, I created and hired for a new role, the Customer Relationship Officer (CRO), whose job was to:

  • capture customer data in the system during sales, so salespeople could focus on the customer
  • coach salespeople on the system in quieter periods, building peer learning and stronger working relationships

2. A structured hiring process

I owned the hiring process from role definition to final recommendation:

  • Job descriptions built on leadership requirements and SOPs
  • Defined interview rounds and qualification criteria
  • Role-specific assessments, built with AI assistance
  • Sourcing candidates, conducting interviews, and evaluating skills against each role's requirements
  • Scheduling follow-up rounds with leadership, followed by a final HR round, where the hiring decision was made

Through this process, 7 team members joined the sales team.

3. Two kinds of training material, each with a clear purpose

SOPs

What to do, when, who is responsible, and the do's and don'ts

L&D curriculum

Why each step matters, how to do it well, and what success looks like

Training started with the full customer journey, then moved to role-specific skills: product knowledge, reading product tags, billing calculations, discount management, and when and how to escalate internally. Linking process and systems this way helped staff understand the reasoning behind their work, not just the steps.

4. Training without disrupting the store

To keep the store running during business hours, the team was split into two training batches using a repeatable session format:

ActivityDuration
SOP + L&D chapter45 minutes
Role-play activity15 minutes
Quiz10 minutes

This was supported by weekly shadow sessions and daily office hours throughout the transition.

5. Performance tracking that drives action

I built performance trackers around the metrics that mattered most for the business:

  • Walk-in conversion ratio
  • Follow-ups completed
  • Data enrichment tasks and Delta Data Fill Rate (the percentage of missing customer details completed for existing customers, tracked per associate and at store level)
  • FJBO % (Future Jewellery Buying Occasions captured, such as upcoming weddings, birthdays and anniversaries, which drive future sales)

These fed performance dashboards (FTD, MTD, YTD) and week-on-week reviews, used to recognise top performers, spot performance gaps early, and put improvement plans in place.

6. Resolving conflict at the source

During Phase 1, friction arose within one team after members missed explaining to customers how rates worked in the digital gold app, part of the customer retention program. When the team asked for help, I:

  1. Stepped in directly with the customer and guided them through the app
  2. Held one-on-ones with the team to understand exactly where their doubts were
  3. Ran an additional training session to close the knowledge gap
  4. Introduced random shadow sessions to check SOP adherence going forward

Treating the conflict as a knowledge gap rather than a blame issue resolved the tension and prevented the same mistake from recurring.

Results

  • New hires became productive within 2 weeks, compared with new hires previously struggling to adapt to the process
  • 27% fewer escalations and more timely follow-ups
  • Improved performance at both individual and store level
  • Performance data enabled a fair rewards and recognition program, with team members awarded based on their sales achievements

Reflection

If I did it again, I would set up baseline measurements for new-hire ramp-up and team performance from day one, so the impact could be quantified even more precisely.
